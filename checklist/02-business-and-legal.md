# 02: Business, legal, and accounts

**Goal:** everything PLAN §8 says to settle before taking real money. This isn't legal advice; bring
in an accountant and a lawyer where noted.

**Done when:** the business exists, Stripe has approved us, and the reviewed policies are published.

## P0: Now (cheap, and protects what we've already built)

- [ ] **(you)** Search "Rip With Me" in the USPTO trademark database (tmsearch.uspto.gov). The plan put
      this before buying the domain. It's still worth doing before we print anything or grow the
      socials.
- [ ] **(you)** Claim matching social handles: Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube. (Also the first step of
      [05](05-hype-and-launch-list.md).)
- [ ] **(you)** Turn on 2FA for Cloudflare, GitHub, the domain registrar, and email. Add Discord and
      Stripe when those accounts exist.

## P1: Before taking money

- [ ] **(you)** Form the business (an LLC is common), get an EIN, and open a business bank account.
- [ ] **(you)** Register for sales tax in our home state, if it has one. Stripe Tax will flag other
      states as sales cross their thresholds.
- [ ] **(you)** Apply to Stripe with an accurate description: we sell specific sealed products and
      open them live with the buyer, who receives the cards. Processors restrict anything that looks
      like a game of chance.
- [ ] **(you)** Have a lawyer review the Terms of Sale, especially:
  - the recording notice (some states, California among them, need everyone on a call to agree);
  - the 30-day rule (D12);
  - the site's "not affiliated with Pokémon" notice, which the audience research suggested an IP
    lawyer look at.
- [ ] **(Claude)** Publish the reviewed Terms, Refund, and Privacy pages
      ([01](01-website.md), P2).
- [ ] **(you)** Start bookkeeping on day one: cost per product, revenue, Stripe fees, and postage per
      order.

## P2: Before the first shipment

- [ ] **(you)** Shipping supplies: a USPS label service, mailers and boxes, penny sleeves, toploaders,
      and labeled bags.
- [ ] **(you)** Set the insurance rule for high-value hits (for example, insure anything over $100).
